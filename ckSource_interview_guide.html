Register guide
Understanding the difference between formal and semi-formal business English in an interview context
What is register?

Register is the level of formality in language. In a job interview, the right register depends on company culture, the seniority of the role, and the dynamic between interviewer and candidate. CKSource operates in a collaborative, engineering-led environment — a semi-formal register is often more effective than rigidly formal language, but formal English is still important in structured assessment and written communication.

Side-by-side comparison
FORMAL BUSINESS
✦ Full sentences, no contractions
✦ Latinate vocabulary
✦ Passive constructions
✦ Hedged, precise language
✦ No idioms or phrasal verbs
✦ Best for: written briefs, executive panels, HR documentation
SEMI-FORMAL
✦ Contractions are natural
✦ Direct, Anglo-Saxon vocabulary
✦ Active voice preferred
✦ Conversational but professional
✦ Some idioms acceptable
✦ Best for: team interviews, culture conversations, video calls
Key vocabulary shifts — formal → semi-formal
F: Could you describe your experience…
S: Can you walk us through…
F: To what extent are you able to…
S: How comfortable are you with…
F: Could you provide an example of a situation in which…
S: Tell us about a time when…
F: Please elaborate on your approach to…
S: Can you say more about how you…
F: What is your perspective on…
S: What do you think about… / What's your take on…
F: utilise / leverage / facilitate
S: use / make use of / help with
At CKSource, the interview culture is collaborative and direct. Semi-formal register signals confidence and approachability. Switch to formal when discussing compensation, legal matters, or writing to multiple stakeholders simultaneously.
Register signals — what to listen for in candidate answers

Strong candidates naturally adapt their register to match the interviewer's. Watch for these signals:

✓ Positive signals
Mirrors your register · Uses precise vocabulary · Structures answers (STAR method) · Avoids jargon overload · Concise and specific
✗ Watch out for
Buzzword-heavy vagueness · Over-formal stiffness · Register mismatch · Casual to the point of imprecision · Generic answers
