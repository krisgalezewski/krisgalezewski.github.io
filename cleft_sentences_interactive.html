What is a cleft sentence?

A cleft sentence splits a simple sentence into two clauses in order to focus on one particular element. The focused element is highlighted — it receives special emphasis — while the rest of the information is pushed into a relative or that-clause. Clefts are common in both spoken and formal written English.

Why use clefts?

Clefts are used to direct the listener's or reader's attention to new, corrective, or contrasting information. They answer implied questions such as "who?", "what?", "when?", or "how?" and are especially effective for emphasis and for correcting misunderstandings.

The six main types
Every cleft sentence has a plain (non-cleft) equivalent. The meaning is the same — the cleft simply changes what is emphasised. Compare: "Maria found the key." → "It was Maria who found the key." (focus on Maria) → "What Maria found was the key." (focus on the key).
