What is inversion?

Inversion means placing the auxiliary verb before the subject — the same order as in a question. When a sentence begins with a negative or restrictive adverbial, this inversion is obligatory in formal written English.

Sentence structure
Negative adverbial+auxiliary+subject+main verb+rest
Never before had I seen such a sight.
Not only did she win, but she also broke the record.
Hardly had we arrived when the storm began.
Which auxiliaries invert?

The same auxiliaries used in question formation: be, have, do/does/did, will, would, can, could, should, must, might, may. If there is no auxiliary, add do/does/did.

The negative adverbial goes at the very front. Everything after it flips: auxiliary → subject → main verb. Never invert the main verb alone ("Never saw I…" ✗).
Normal vs inverted
Normal orderI had never seen such a sight before.
InvertedNever before had I seen such a sight.
Common mistake ✗Never before I had seen such a sight.
With do/does/did
Normal orderShe rarely speaks in public.
InvertedRarely does she speak in public.
Common mistake ✗Rarely she speaks in public.
