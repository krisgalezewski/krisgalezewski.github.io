Part 1 — Types of orders

OK, so I'll start by explaining the types of orders that come through to us. For Tiny, they are either renewals — people just renewing their regular orders — or new orders, which means a brand new customer. Then we have expansions, which is people expanding on what they already have — so it could be more seats that people are using. An upsell is when they're either being upsold from self-service to sales-assisted, or they could have an upsell to what they already have — so maybe they bought one product and now they're adding another.

Part 2 — Processing a renewal

It comes to you when it's submitted to finance. They can't make any changes to this once it's submitted. So if there's any changes that you find — errors — you have to reject it, send it back to them and have them fix it and resubmit it again.

The first thing I do is check the subscription dates for a renewal, because you want to make sure that the dates aren't overlapping and they have the correct year. So I look at the transaction tab and I check out the dates on the contract. This is automatically set up for us and it takes this information to populate this field, but it has to be under the products field. So if this is empty or blank, you have to reject it and have them add the dates in.

I quickly scan the boilerplate information and the pricing details — it's pretty much the same all the time. If there was any discount terms, additional terms, notes to finance, if this was really long, then I would read it again.

Part 3 — Checking backup documentation

For us to be able to bill a customer, we need certain things. We need either a signed quote with them accepting our payment terms, or if the customer emails us saying that they accept our quote with the quote number in it, we can accept that as an email acceptance.

The other thing is if a customer gives a purchase order — there are a couple of ways we can accept it. Even though they've accepted our terms in the past, when they have a purchase order, most people's purchase orders say "by accepting our purchase order, you accept our terms." So we need to override that. We can accept the purchase order as long as they reference our quote number on it — because whatever wording we have in our quote supersedes whatever they have for terms in their purchase order.

If they were a reseller, this would say yes and it would tell you who the reseller was. A reseller is a third party that sells our product on to an end user. If it was a multi-year deal and we were billing them upfront, this is supposed to be checked off. The net 30 days — that means they have 30 days from the invoice date to pay us.

Part 4 — Tax and address

It's always important to make sure we get the correct ship-to address because the sales tax is based on where it's shipped to — because the shipping address is the person that's using the software. We collect VAT in the EU, but at this point in time we do not collect VAT in the UK, only in the EU. We do not collect VAT in the UK — only in the EU.

So we try to avoid errors going into NetSuite at all costs. Once it syncs, you don't want that to happen. If there's an error, you don't want it to go into NetSuite because once it gets into NetSuite you can't fix it in Maxio — you have to fix it there. You could always credit the invoice and start over again, but if you have an invoice with ten lines, it's pretty time-consuming.

Part 5 — Approving and syncing

So I'm going to go in and I'm going to approve it. This is where at the top of the page you see the finance approval history. I can approve it here or I can reject it here. If I reject it, it brings up a box and you can put a note in why you're rejecting it.

Now that I approved it, I can go into Maxio and sync Salesforce right here. It should bring it in on the sales order tab. Here's the portal account email — the portal account email. We process it — I just hover over it and we process it.

The cadence is the primary invoice cadence that is setting up when reminder emails go to the customer. We allow people to pay by credit card for anything five thousand dollars and under. If a customer wants to pay by credit card after the fact and it's over five thousand, I do a Stripe link for them and charge them a three-percent fee.

Part 6 — Committing sales tax and sending the invoice

I know that it's in a state that we collect in New York, so I am going to commit sales tax for them. This goes to Avalara and it's showing you the sales tax address. Now you can see the sales taxes are in here and they have a link to go to the sales tax.

Now I'm going to view the invoice. I go into the BCC and I put in all of the account managers so they all get a copy of it. I send the E-bill — and you can see right now it says your E-bill activity today. You have to weed through a lot of emails through there because there's a ton of junk. I save all the backup — the invoice, the email approval, the quote — so when we have an audit, I have all that data right there.

Part 7 — New deals and upsells

For any upsells or any new deals that come through, I make sure that the portal account email is not already in there. Have all our ducks in a row — that's what we want before we approve anything. So say if someone came through and said this is a new deal, I would look at this email and go into Maxio and search by portal account email and paste it in there. If it's telling me that portal email account's already in use, I know it's not a new customer because it's already being used.

A proof of concept (POC) means the customer wants to see if the software is going to work for their situation — it's always zero dollars. New deals we're supposed to process within 24 hours. Renewals we're supposed to process within 48 hours. So everyone's always like, did you get to this yet? — it's very time-consuming work and I get pulled into meetings on top of it.

Part 8 — Deferred revenue and filing

I save everything in fiscal year 2026, deferred revenue backup. I get rid of the PDF name and rename it as invoice and save it, then I go into the email that I get and I say "hello all" and I paste it in and just put "has been billed" so everyone knows that it has been billed. If it was a signed quote, I would put "signed quote". And then when we have an audit, I have all that data right there — the invoice, the approval to bill them.

Once it's closed — once it's Closed Won — it's locked. No one can touch it. That's how you do renewals. Expansions are pretty much the same — they're basically renewals. There's not much difference.
